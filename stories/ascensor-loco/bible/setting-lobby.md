# Setting: The Lobby

**ID**: lobby  
**Name**: The Lobby (Ground Floor)  

## Description

The lobby is the building's official face—a space designed to project authority and permanence. High ceilings, marble or tile flooring worn smooth by decades of footsteps, dim lighting from fixtures that have not been updated in generations. The walls are painted a institutional green or beige, covered with notices, directories, and official announcements that have accumulated and been only partially removed, leaving ghostly traces of past information. A front desk faces the entrance, with a bell and a ledger. Behind the desk, a doorway leads to administrative offices.

## Atmosphere

The lobby is mundane and oppressive in equal measure. It is the interface between the outside world and the building's interior, designed to be ordinary enough that visitors feel no alarm, yet formal enough that they remember they are under official authority. The air is cool and still, as though time moves differently here. There is a faint smell of cleaning products, old paper, and stone. Echoes are absorbed by the dead space, creating a sense of isolation despite the open architecture. The lobby is where rules begin to apply.

## Narrative Function

The lobby is the story's threshold—the boundary between the rational world and the impossible. Every scene in the lobby represents a moment of decision or revelation between what is known and what is hidden. It is where Ana enters and where she will exit. It is where she first meets Miguel and where she returns to confront the building's authorities. The lobby's very ordinariness makes the impossible more shocking by contrast.
