# Character: Miguel Sánchez

## Header

**ID**: miguel-sanchez  
**Name**: Miguel Sánchez  
**Role**: The Elevator Operator and Gatekeeper  

**Arc**: Miguel is a man caught between worlds—he knows the building's secrets but is bound by a duty to protect them, or possibly bound by fear and complicity. He appears helpful and mysterious in equal measure, offering guidance while withholding crucial information. As Ana pushes deeper, Miguel's loyalties are tested. By the end, he must reveal whether he is guardian, jailer, or victim himself.

**Initial State**: Mysterious, helpful, enigmatic—the insider with hidden knowledge  
**Final State**: Revealed as either protector or captor; his true allegiance exposed

**Desire**: To preserve the building's equilibrium and the secrets it holds  
**Fear**: That someone outside the building will learn the truth and destroy what has been carefully maintained

## Body

**Appearance**: Miguel is in his fifties, with silver threading through dark hair and a face marked by decades of the same small routines. He has the bearing of someone who has lived his entire life in one place and has made peace with that confinement. His eyes are kind but guarded, as though he is always calculating what to reveal.

**Costume**: Miguel wears the uniform of an elevator operator—a gray or navy jacket with small brass buttons, a name tag worn so long it has become part of his skin. His clothing is immaculate; his appearance is his anchor to normalcy and professionalism.

**Voice**: Miguel speaks with the careful precision of someone who has learned to say nothing while appearing to say everything. He uses long pauses, allows silences, and deflects with courtesy. As pressure mounts, his voice takes on weight and sadness—the voice of someone bearing a burden too heavy to share.

**Narrative Function**: Miguel is the story's moral ambiguity. Ana (and the reader) cannot decide whether he is a hero protecting the building or a villain protecting his complicity. He represents the difficult truth that good people are sometimes trapped in bad systems and can become enablers. His scenes with Ana create the story's central tension: how do you trust someone who may be deceiving you, or confess to someone who may report you?

**K1–K7 Thread**: Miguel appears in pages 1, 2, 3, 4, and 5. Page 1: introduction as helper and mystery. Page 2: absent but his rules are felt. Page 3: confronted by Ana, forced to explain the elevator's nature. Page 4: guides Ana deeper, testing her commitment. Page 5: revealed as part of the power structure or as a prisoner within it.
