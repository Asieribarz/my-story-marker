# Setting: The Rooftop

**ID**: rooftop  
**Name**: The Rooftop (The Heights)  

## Description

The rooftop is a large, open space covered in gravel and worn stone, with the city or landscape visible at the horizon. Metal railings run along the edges, and various structures dot the space—ventilation shafts, water tanks, old equipment whose purpose is unclear. There are benches, a small shelter, and evidence of long habitation: a garden in containers, weathered tables, personal items suggesting someone has made a life here. The railings offer views of the surrounding world, but the perspective is strange—the city below looks distant and unreal, as though the rooftop belongs to a different layer of existence than the world beyond. The light here is open and direct, the sky visible, the stars visible at night.

## Atmosphere

The rooftop is the story's space of clarity and elevation—where sight and perspective are clearest. It is separated from the building's interior oppression, exposed to the sky and elements. There is wind here, bringing sounds from outside. The air is fresh, different from the stale air of the building below. Despite the openness, there is a sense of isolation—the rooftop is far from help, far from intervention, far from ordinary life. It is a liminal space where revelation can happen because there is nowhere to hide, no institutional structures to protect lies. The rooftop is beautiful and terrifying, offering freedom and exposure in equal measure.

## Narrative Function

The rooftop is where the story's final revelation occurs, where Ana meets Rosa and learns the truth of the building's design. It is the highest point, metaphorically and physically, where Ana can see farthest and understand most completely. Scenes on the rooftop are moments of absolute clarity, where the building's mystery is resolved, and Ana must make her final choice. The rooftop is where Ana can most easily communicate with the outside world, making it the space where she must decide whether she will do so. It is simultaneously the place of greatest freedom and greatest constraint.
