# Setting: The Elevator Interior

**ID**: elevator  
**Name**: The Elevator (Interior/Shaft)  

## Description

The elevator is a small, enclosed space—roughly six feet by eight feet—with walls that appear to be dark wood or metal, worn smooth by countless hands. The floor is polished brass or steel, its surface worn in a specific pattern by standing passengers. A control panel on one wall holds brass buttons for each floor, though the numbering does not correspond to the building's official floors. A mechanical clock above the doors shows the time, but its accuracy is uncertain. The doors are brass, covered with a patina of age and touched hands. The ceiling shows a maintenance panel, and faint wiring is visible. The space smells of metal, old oil, and something else—something that might be incense or might be time itself.

## Atmosphere

The elevator is liminal and surreal. Time behaves strangely here—journeys that should take seconds feel like hours, and hours feel like minutes. The space is simultaneously confined and infinite; the small box seems to contain more space than its dimensions should allow. There is a humming sound, faint and constant, that might be mechanical or might be something else. The air is cool and slightly metallic. Passengers emerge from it disoriented, uncertain of time, questioning reality. The elevator is where the impossible becomes unavoidable.

## Narrative Function

The elevator is the story's central engine—the machine that drives all action and revelation. Every journey inside it represents a crossing of threshold, a moment where safety is left behind and truth approaches. The elevator is the building's secret made manifest. Scenes inside the elevator create moments of suspended time where characters are forced to confront the impossible and each other. The elevator's behavior embodies the story's central mystery: how can something impossible be real?
