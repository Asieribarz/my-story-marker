# Character: Rosa Mendez

## Header

**ID**: rosa-mendez  
**Name**: Rosa Mendez  
**Role**: The Architect and Keeper of Origins  

**Arc**: Rosa designed the elevator deliberately, and the building around it. She is the source of the building's impossible geometry. She appears first as a name in records, then as a ghost or as a living presence on the rooftop where she has retreated. She holds the key to understanding whether the building is sanctuary or prison, liberation or entrapment. Her revelation is the story's final truth—the answer to why the elevator was designed this way at all.

**Initial State**: Present only as a name in documents, a memory, a shadow of authority from the past  
**Final State**: Revealed as the architect's conscience, the designer bearing witness to what she created

**Desire**: To ensure that the truth of her design survives, and to pass responsibility to someone who will choose wisely  
**Fear**: That her creation has become corrupted into something other than what she intended

## Body

**Appearance**: Rosa is elderly, possibly in her eighties, though her exact age is unclear. She has the appearance of someone who has lived long in solitude—her face is lined with thought, her eyes bright with intelligence that has nowhere to go. She moves slowly but with purpose, as though every motion has been considered many times over.

**Costume**: Rosa wears simple, worn clothing—layers of neutral tones that suggest she dresses not for appearance but for function. A long cardigan over worn hands. Comfortable shoes worn through years of pacing. On her wrist, a single bracelet made of brass or gold—delicate and deliberate, suggesting some past elegance.

**Voice**: Rosa speaks with the voice of someone who has had decades to think through every word she might say. Her speech is measured, poetic sometimes, precise in its language. She does not speak often, and when she does, each word carries weight. There is sadness in her voice, and something like resignation—the voice of someone who has accepted responsibility for consequences beyond her control.

**Narrative Function**: Rosa represents the story's moral center and its deepest mystery. She is the source of the impossible, the designer who made the elevator knowing it would create secrets and danger. Her choice to design it this way—whatever that choice was—is the final reversal. She passes the burden of that choice to Ana, making clear that knowing the truth is not the end of the story, but the beginning of responsibility.

**K1–K7 Thread**: Rosa appears only in page 5 (as a name, a clue in documents) and page 6 (as a presence, a revelation). Her scenes with Ana are the story's climax—the moment when motivation and consequence come together, and Ana must decide what to do with the knowledge Rosa offers.
