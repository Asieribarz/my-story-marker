name: Elena's apartment — refuge and mirror
---

**Appearance.** A small one-bedroom apartment in a working-class Madrid neighborhood. The living room is also the dining and sleeping area when Marco stays. The walls hold maps of multiple countries, a large mirror, and a small shelf of objects from travels—a Moroccan jar, a Peru guidebook, a photo of two women laughing. The kitchen is efficient and clean. Everything has a place and a purpose.

**Atmosphere.** Orderly and thoughtful, but not sterile. This is a home built by someone who has learned that safety requires organization, but meaning requires memory. The apartment feels both temporary and permanent—Elena has lived here for fifteen years but it retains the quality of a way station. Marco's arrival tests whether it is a refuge or just another place he will eventually have to leave.

**Function.** The only stable shelter Marco has; a space that reveals Elena's own history and offers lessons in survival
