# Premise expansion

**Premise (run argument).** A Peruvian architect arrives in Madrid on a signed
contract that no longer exists, and has to find out who he is without the job his
family believes he holds.

## Central conflict

Marco arrives in Madrid with a signed contract for a prestigious architecture firm. On his second day, he learns the firm has folded—the job evaporates. Stranded with a tourist visa and a family in Lima expecting his success, Marco must choose between revealing the truth and inventing a new identity in a city that doesn't know him.

## Theme

**Who are we without the roles we were promised?** The story explores how identity becomes tethered to achievement, and what remains when that achievement is stripped away. Marco's true journey is not rescuing the job, but discovering that his worth exists independently of external validation.

## Tone, as interpreted for this story

Realistic and intimate, grounded in the mundane indignities of starting over. Moments of quiet humor. A tone that respects the character's dignity while unflinching about the cost of displacement. Not triumphalist—the ending offers acceptance, not rescue.

## Tentative ending

Marco stops searching for the architecture job that defines him. He finds modest, honest work and a small community of other displaced people in Madrid. He calls his family with a different story than he planned to tell—one that is true. The final image: Marco, walking through a neighborhood he now knows, no longer looking for the person he was supposed to be.

---

**Story shape:** 8 pages across 3 chapters, three acts of setup (conflict emergence), development (reinvention and resistance), and resolution (acceptance).
