Marco has been in Madrid for three months, working illegal construction jobs under foreman supervision. His reputation grows—his architectural knowledge makes him valuable as a site supervisor. He earns more than simple laborers but remains part of the undeclared economy, invisible to Spanish employment records. Javier remains his closest friend, teaching him the philosophy of surviving day-to-day without planning beyond immediate needs.

Seeking legitimacy, Marco attempts to navigate the Spanish immigration system. He visits the Oficina de Extranjería and meets Miguel Ferrero, a bureaucrat who systematically explains that there is no legal path for Marco: no shortage in architecture, no family in Spain, no asylum grounds. Every avenue the system offers leads back to the same conclusion: deportation or return to Peru. Marco receives an official notice demanding he leave Spain within thirty days. This bureaucratic rejection becomes a turning point—he accepts that the system will never grant him legitimacy and stops expecting it to save him.

The chapter ends with Marco fully committed to illegal permanence in Madrid. His visa is expired. Deportation is legally justified. But in accepting this reality, he discovers an unexpected freedom: he can stop believing in the structures that reject him and begin building a life outside them.
