The light came up behind them and Kethra stood out of the white all at once, forty flat roofs on the only rise there was.

Nothing moved on it. They came up through the ring of houses with the sun a finger off the crust and the beasts going short, and Sev saw what was wrong before she had a word for it. Every door in the town was barred on the outside. Every one. The bars were in their brackets and the brackets were sound and the dust lay even along the tops of them.

"Nobody does that to a town," Oduin said, to the camels. "A town does that to itself, going out."

The windlass stood over the wellhead with its rope coiled and made up. Sev dropped a stone down it and the well answered wrong: not the fall and the knock of a dry shaft, and not water either, but a flat slap close under her, like a hand laid on a table.

The child was already going along the wall to the tally house.

It was one room against the wellhead. The keeper was at the table with his back to the door and his head down on his arm, and he had been there some days, and there was a cup by his hand with a white crust dried in the bottom of it.

Sev did not touch him. She went round the table to see the book.

The ledger lay open under his other arm at the foot of a page, and the last thing written in it was three lines in a steady hand, sworn on such a day, the apprentice of this town's count, and then the name of the child standing beside her.

Under it the season's column was ruled and empty.

He had gone on drinking it. She had stopped.

"Where is the tally?" Sev said.

The child put her hand flat on the case inside her shirt and held it there.
