The slab took the three of them and a rope to shift, and the shaft under it was dark and cold and smelled of water, and the seal on the cistern below was whole, with the year and the Office stamp in the wax.

They drank standing at the shaft mouth. Then they filled what they could carry, and set the slab back over the shaft and weighted it, because a cistern left open is brine inside the day.

By then the sun was a hand up and the crust had begun to let go. The nearer camel went in to the knee at the second step and stood there with its head down, not fighting it, which is worse.

Oduin looked at that for a while.

Then he went and cut the loads off both beasts and let them down into the salt, and turned the camels loose, light, and put them away from him towards the landing with the flat of his hand.

"They will come in or they will not," he said. "They will not come in carrying."

Nobody priced it.

They went the rest of the way on their own legs, which is the only thing that moves on the Pan after sunrise, wet to the knee and then wet to the thigh, with the child between them and the water on their backs. It took the whole of the morning. Twice the crust let Sev through to the hip and twice Oduin had her out of it before she had finished going.

The dock hands saw them first, a long way out, and came down to the foot of the ramp and stood there, twenty or thirty of them, and did not shout or ask anything. They were the ones who had walked in off the Pan out of Kethra two seasons ago and been entered lapsed, and they knew what it looked like from the inside.

Somebody took the casks. Somebody put a coat on the child.

Sev went up the ramp with an hour of the morning left and pushed the Office door open.

Behind the lectern the Kethra nail was empty, and the only paper on the board under it was an order striking Cairn Four from the line for repair, dated the night they went out.
