They lay up through the middle of the day under the tarpaulin with the beasts couched and the crust too hot to put a flat hand on, and drank what the day cost them and not a mouthful more. Nine had come and gone in the night behind them, dry, the slab still lying square beside its shaft where nobody had moved it.

At full dark they got up and went on, two thirds of a window to Cairn Four, on the child's count.

There is nothing to say about that walk. The lamp reached twenty paces and the twenty paces were white, and beyond them was the same white without the lamp on it, and the only thing in the world that knew where they were was a girl with no voice walking at the near shoulder of the first camel.

Sev counted aloud from the halfway of it and did not know she was doing it. Oduin heard her and said nothing, which was a kindness.

Then the child stopped.

She stood with the can in both hands and she did not go on, and after a moment she put one foot forward and set it down flat, twice, on the place.

Sev brought the lamp round. Twenty paces of white. No cone. No whitewash taking the light back. No slab, no shadow, no heap of blocks the height of a standing man.

"Count it again."

The child tapped the can once.

"Could you be short a thousand? It is a long stage and you are carrying."

The child went down and wrote the number in the salt, the same number, and left it there.

"Could the wind have set you off the line in the dark? It has done it to better than you."

Nothing at all. No tap, no hand, no writing. She stood on the place she had put her foot on and looked at Sev and waited for Sev to be finished.

Under all three of them, very faintly, the crust began to tick, the way it does when the cold is going out of it.
