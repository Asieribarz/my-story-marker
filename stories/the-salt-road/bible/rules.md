# World rules

r1. Nothing on the Pan is supernatural: every apparent marvel has a physical, tidal or human cause.
r2. The salt crust bears weight only while it is cold. It holds from full dark until the sun stands a hand above the horizon, and after that it softens over the brine beneath and will not carry a loaded animal. A person on their own legs can still cross it, wet to the knee and slow, and after sunrise that is the only way anything moves on the Pan.
r3. There is no water on the Pan but what a party carries and what is held in the two cisterns of the line, cut under Cairn Four and Cairn Nine and under no other cairn. A cistern broken open and left unsealed is brine within a day.
r4. The Pan is flat and white and holds no landmark of its own. Off the line of cairns there is nothing to steer by but what people have left out there, and one cairn cannot be seen from the last except in the hour of first light.
r5. Tally law: a well-town holds its cistern line only while it lodges a water tally at the Salt Office each season. Three seasons unlodged and the line lapses, when the book closes at noon on the last tally-day, to whoever last drew from it. A tally-case broken open anywhere but on the Office lectern is void as a lodgement.
r6. Brine takes the voice before it takes anything else, and takes it for good. A brine-drinker who stops drinking keeps the rest; one who does not stop does not keep anything, and nobody has ever got the voice back.
