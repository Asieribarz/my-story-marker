Tallow broke Kethra's seal herself in the tally house and the case was empty: void as a lodgement under article five, and the count nowhere but in her. She wrote on the table that the keeper had sent the case out empty because the last two tallies never reached the lectern, and that the case was the thing he meant them to take. Sev told her she could not stay in the town she had reached that morning. They lifted the tally-house floor and loaded Kethra's last sealed cistern, water as far as Cairn Four and not a swallow past it, and went out on to the crust at full dark with Kethra's ledger under the child's arm. The draws she wrote into it were water Oduin had loaded at the ramp head in the spring, under an Office stamp, with the standing board bare; he gave back the forty the Office paid him to be slow and asked for his name in the book beside the count. Past a dry Cairn Nine the count ran out where she said it must, no cairn standing at it, and she would not take the number back.
