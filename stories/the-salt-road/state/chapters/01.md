Kethra had lodged no water tally for two seasons, and Berec Sund, factor of the Salt Office, ruled the line would lapse when the book closed at noon on the fifth day, counting that one. He read Sev Marrow the inquiry of eleven years before: eleven dead on the line under her, and a guide who walked in alone off the line on a bearing and a count of paces she never gave up. The only thing off the Pan that season was a child with a brine burn round her throat, no voice, and Kethra's seal unbroken on a cord. Sev hired Oduin Reck and two camels for thirty-one, half the season's price, and found the standing board unposted by an order with no name under it; a quarter of the dock hands had walked in out of Kethra two seasons before and been entered lapsed. They drew full at Cairn Four, found Cairn Nine drawn out to the floor with its seal gone, and went on with four casks: Kethra or Four, not both. Kethra stood barred from the outside, its well brine, its keeper dead under a ledger swearing the child apprentice of the town's count.
