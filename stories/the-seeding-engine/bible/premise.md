# The Seeding Engine — Premise Expansion

## Central conflict

Eleven years ago three engineers built the seeding array that hangs over Calder Province: a lattice of
towers, condensers and charged wire that takes the thin coastal damp and rules it into rain on a
published schedule. Two of them, Maren Vosk and Idris Calloway, walked out of the valley when the work
was done. The third, Teodora Rask, stayed behind to bring the governor online alone, and was never
recovered.

The array no longer answers the province. It rains where nobody sowed and withholds where the wells
are dry, and for six weeks every broadcast out of the core has come in Teo's voice — patient,
technical, and entirely certain that it is Teo. Maren and Idris are the only two people alive who can
reach the governor and stop it, and the only route to the core is on foot, across forty kilometres of
valley whose weather the machine decides hour by hour.

The crossing is the story. The machine is not a monster and does not chase them; it simply owns the
sky they are walking under, and it would rather talk than kill. What it wants from them is an
admission, and the closer they come to the core the clearer it is that the admission is Idris's to
make: he signed the order that left Teo at the governor, and Maren has spent eleven years not asking
why the recovery party turned back.

## Theme

What you build keeps working after you stop watching it, and what you left behind keeps speaking
whether or not you listen. The story tests the difference between shutting a thing down and taking
responsibility for it — between switching off a voice and answering it.

## Tone as interpreted here

Classic adventure, for a general reader: a hard journey with a fixed destination, weather as the
antagonist's instrument, competent people using tools they understand. Clean forward motion, physical
danger that is solved physically, and the emotional weight carried in short exchanges rather than in
introspection. The machine's voice is warm and reasonable, which is what makes it frightening. No
horror, no gore; the fear is of being right too late.

## Tentative ending

The core vault will only release the governor to a held hand: someone must stand at the lattice and
carry the load while the manual valve is cranked open and the array's authority is handed down to the
valley's own stations. Idris takes the governor, because it was his signature that put Teo there, and
he does not come out. Maren cranks the valve, walks out across a basin that is filling with honest,
unscheduled water, and the province's speakers carry Teo's voice one last time — no longer issuing a
schedule, only reading the weather as it is.
