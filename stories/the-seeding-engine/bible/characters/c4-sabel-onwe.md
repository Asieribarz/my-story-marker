name: Sabel Onwe
role: Ally; well-keeper of the drowned orchard, the last inhabitant on the array's route
desire: To keep her orchard's wells alive through whatever the engineers are about to do to the sky
fear: That the two strangers will shut the machine off and leave a province with no rain at all
arc: Begins as a wary householder who has learned to live by the array's published schedule and who
    treats the engineers as the authors of her whole difficult life — she helps them only to find out
    what they intend. In the middle of the crossing she gives up the one thing she has been hoarding,
    the manual valve crank salvaged from the old works, because she decides the risk of trusting them
    is smaller than another year of scheduled water. Ends having chosen the valley's future on the
    engineers' word, and is the first person Maren has to face with the result.
---

**Appearance.** Sixty, small, weather-cured, with a broad flat face and eyes permanently narrowed
against glare. Hands stained dark to the wrist from orchard sap and well iron. Walks with a limp she
does not favour and carries more than she should out of habit. Grey hair wound tight under a cloth.

**Costume.** Layered, patched, practical: a rain cape of stitched condenser fabric scavenged from a
fallen tower, a shift the colour of dust, rope-soled shoes for wet clay. A brass whistle on a cord for
calling the goats, and a ring of well keys heavy enough to be a weapon.

**Voice.** Dry, concrete, faintly mocking. She talks in what things cost — litres, seasons, trees lost —
and answers grand statements with a question about the next fortnight. She has no patience for
apology and says so. When she finally offers help she does it curtly, as a transaction, so that nobody
has to be thanked.
