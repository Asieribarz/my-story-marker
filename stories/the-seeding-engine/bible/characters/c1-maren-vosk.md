name: Maren Vosk
role: Protagonist; hydraulics engineer, co-designer of the seeding array
desire: To reach the core and put the valley's weather back into the valley's hands
fear: That the eleven years she spent not asking what happened to Teo were a choice, not an accident
arc: Begins as a technician who believes the problem is a machine fault and that a correct procedure, correctly executed, settles any question — she has kept her conscience quiet by keeping her hands busy. Through the crossing she is forced to hear the voice as a person, to learn what her partner signed, and to accept that the array cannot be switched off, only taken responsibility for. Ends as the one who chooses what the valley gets instead of the array, cranks the valve alone, and walks out carrying both the work and the debt in the open.
---

**Appearance.** Forty-three, tall and narrow through the shoulders, with the stooped, economical walk of
someone who has spent a career in service tunnels. Grey coming in hard at the temples, hair cut short
because long hair catches in machinery. Deep-set eyes, a broken nose set badly, hands scarred across
the knuckles from valve work. A long burn scar up the inside of her left forearm from the
commissioning year that she never explains.

**Costume.** Oiled canvas field coat over a technician's jumpsuit, both the faded blue of the Calder
works. Heavy boots, gaiters against the salt. A wide tool harness carrying a valve crank, a torque
bar, a pressure gauge and a coil of sealing wire — everything she owns is a tool she can name. Goggles
pushed up on her forehead, a folded paper valley chart in her breast pocket that she trusts more than
any relay.

**Voice.** Short declaratives, technical nouns, no ornament. She answers questions with measurements
and deflects feeling with procedure: "Pressure's up. Move." When she does say something personal it
arrives flat and late, as if she has been carrying it for a kilometre. She talks to machinery more
easily than to people, and the machine notices.
