name: Kellin Ridge Station and the Pass
---

**Appearance.** A squat concrete relay station bolted to the lip of the ridge that closes Calder
Province on its northern side, one storey of instrument room over a cable gallery, its roof a thicket
of aerials and a single condenser vane. Below it the Kellin Pass switchbacks down two hundred metres
of scree to the valley floor, cut on one side by the old spillway channel — a dry concrete trough
wide enough to drive a truck along, with sluice gates at the top that have not been opened in eleven
years.

**Atmosphere.** The last place with a floor, a kettle and a working relay. Dry wind, the ticking of
cooling metal, the smell of hot dust and cable insulation. From the doorway the whole province is
visible at once as a pale basin under a sky ruled into weather like fields: rain hanging in a tidy
column over one orchard, hard sunlight over everything else. It feels less like a frontier post than
like the top of a staircase into somebody else's house.

**Narrative function.** The departure point and the story's only line back to the outside world. It
establishes the array, the schedule, the voice on the relay speaker and the fact that everything below
is governed. When the spillway runs and the pass goes under water, this is what is lost: the station
is the measure of how far the crossing has become one-way.
