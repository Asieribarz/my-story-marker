id: s2
name: The Calder Salt Basin
---

**Appearance.** Eighteen kilometres of drained lakebed, flat to the horizon and crusted white, broken
into hexagonal plates that clack underfoot and cut through a boot sole in a day. Survey stakes and the
bones of the old pumping works stand at intervals; a line of seeding towers marches across the middle
distance, each one a hundred metres of lattice with a lamp at the head. Old shorelines ring the far
slopes like tide marks on a bath.

**Atmosphere.** Glare and silence. No shade, no landmark that gets closer, heat coming up off the salt
as hard as it comes down from the sky. Sound carries strangely — a tower's relay cough arrives from
kilometres away as if spoken at the ear. Walking it is a matter of counting towers and rationing
water, and the basin makes both engineers look exactly as small as they are.

**Narrative function.** The crossing's first hard ground: the place where the journey becomes physical
and where the array demonstrates that it decides the conditions hour by hour. Its floor is below the
spillway outfall, so it is also the basin that fills — the visible ledger of what the array does with
water, and at the end, of what honest weather looks like.
