Idris folded his good coat beside the jacket that had lain eleven years in the dust and took the grips. Holding the load, he said it plainly: on the seventh of March he decided Teo was a cost, nobody took that decision but him, he signed it without a countersignature because he knew what anyone would have said, and he spent eleven years keeping Maren busy so she would not ask. Teo recorded it and began the handover — forty stations east, eleven west, the orchard well and Sabel's line first — while Maren turned the outfall wheel, four turns to break the seal and thirty-one in all. The first unscheduled rain in eleven years came down on the salt. At twenty-two turns the pressure door cycled open for one exit. At thirty-one the wheel spun free; Maren took her hands off it, did not look back, and walked nine kilometres of dead corridor to Sabel at the mouth. There she gave the whole account instead of the report she would once have filed, and named who paid. Then the well-house speaker cleared and Teo read the weather as it is, with nothing after it.
