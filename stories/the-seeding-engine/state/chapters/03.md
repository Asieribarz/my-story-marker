The corridor was nine kilometres of raised steel grating, under hail timed to the quarter hour. At the third kilometre Idris said his hands were going, and Maren split the crank into a thirty-kilo shaft and an eighty-kilo gear case to leapfrog it in two loads, giving him the part she could not replace. Twice her boot went through a plate and twice he had her arm before she asked. At the ninth kilometre the hail stopped four minutes early, the wire went silent, and the pressure door stood open before they reached it. Inside, it cycled shut and Teo spoke from the room instead of the towers. The lattice stood above a low steel frame with two hand grips worn smooth, a folded jacket on the floor, eleven years of dust. The handover is thirty-one steps and she will give them all of it — but the lattice will not hand the valley to an empty room. It holds or a hand holds. They cannot take the weather back and both walk out. Maren seated the crank in the outfall socket; behind her, Idris set his case down beside the cradle.
