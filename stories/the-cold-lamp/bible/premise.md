# Premise expansion

**Premise (run argument).** A young sailor who fears deep water crosses a storm-blown
archipelago to find her brother, missing since the lighthouse went dark.

## Central conflict

Mara Vell must cross the deepest water in the Banks, in the worst nine days of the storm
season, to reach a lighthouse that has stopped burning and find the brother who kept it.
Against her stands the warden of Gannet Isle, who is paid by wreck law for everything the
sea puts ashore and is therefore richer with the light out than with it lit. Against her
also stands the thing she learns halfway across: her brother is not a victim waiting to be
carried home. He put the light out himself.

## Theme

A light is kept for people you will never meet. Courage is not the absence of the fear; it
is standing the watch with the fear still in you.

## Tone, as interpreted for this story

Classic adventure: plain, concrete, forward-moving prose. Weather and tide are the first
antagonists and are never metaphorical. Danger is physical and legible - cold, depth, a
making tide, a short count of oil. No irony, no knowingness, no comic relief at the expense
of the stakes. Sentences get shorter as the water gets closer.

## Tentative ending

Mara goes down the Drowned Stair into the deep water she has refused all her life and
brings the lens up. The claim on the keeper's oil is lodged inside the nine days, in her
brother's own hand, which voids the warden's title to it. The lamp-wright who has refused
the oath for two years takes it and relights the Cold Lamp. The first sweep of the new beam
finds the warden's lugger still in the channel, and she is held under the article she used.
Mara stays on Calder Rock as relief keeper and stands the first watch.
