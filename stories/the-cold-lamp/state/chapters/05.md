The claim went in with two hours of the fourteenth left on it. Halloran Keeve, who had rung the wreck bell and stood by a ketch for two hours off a lee shore, crossed the Weathering with Mara on the four o'clock slack, told her without a proverb in it what the note of hand had really bought - boats kept off the east crossing, not money - and paid the note off out of the salvage line in front of witnesses. The clerk would not take the claim without the keeper's own hand. Tem signed it in the watch room knowing it named him keeper on the days he stripped the light, and undertook to give the article eleven direction and everything after it to the roll. The eleven casks came back. Nell Carrow trued the lens on its bed, rebuilt the mechanism, filled the reservoir out of the first cask and said the keeper's words out loud for the first time in nine years, and the first sweep caught an unlit lugger in the channel. Ester Quill was taken off it under article four, because a claim allowed inside the nine days had taken the casks out of her wreck goods. The service sent no relief for eleven days, and Mara stood the watch.
