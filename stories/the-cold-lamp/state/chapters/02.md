Mara and Halloran crossed the Weathering on the four o'clock slack, over the deepest ground in the archipelago, and the hour ran out under them: the channel turned over white the whole two miles back to Ossary, and the next slack was four o'clock the following day. They landed on Calder Rock after dark and found the tower door unbarred and a wet track leading in and up, in prints smaller than Mara's own. The tower was empty. Searched room by room it gave up nothing broken and nothing taken - a bunk made up square, a mug of tea days cold - a place walked out of rather than emptied by force. In the lantern room the lens had been wiped down and squared true in its frame, and the reservoir under it was dry to the brass, which meant the lamp had not failed but had been put out. The keeper's log gave the rest: three strokes rung on Gannet on the first, a death bell, and her brother's own hand going on writing in the book until the ninth, eight days after the Banks were told he was dead.
