La Alondra came into Cadiz seven days past her bond, out of a calm that had held her a fortnight off Trafalgar, with an empty hold and a lead tank of stale water nobody had emptied, and the news was all over the fish quay before the warps were on the ring.

It went the way such news goes.

The rope-walk woman said she had sold him nine pounds of hair and that was the only fact in it. A man off a Sanlucar boat said the captain of the Alondra had gone mad in the Gulf and thrown a woman overboard, and was made to take it back, and took it back. Two of the crew told it straight in three taverns and were bought drinks for it, which is what a town does to a story it has decided about. By Lent it had become a thing men said to each other about Ignacio Robles when he was out of the room, and the shape it had was: *eleven years, and nothing to show.*

Which was exact, and he never once disputed it.

Fausto Delgado took the ship, as he was entitled to, and sold her to somebody who fished, as he had said he would. He never spoke of the voyage in Seville or anywhere else; the hall behind the Lonja was let to a man selling Flemish glass. That is all anybody knows about what Don Fausto made of it, and it is probably not nothing.

Brites Olaya went first mate into a Genoa trader and grew her plait back, and would answer any question about the voyage except one.

And Pau kept the rope.

He had the two cut ends of it in a tin box for sixty years, and he showed it to people, and what they saw was some cut hair in a box, because that is what it was.

They found Ignacio at the head of the slip one evening in the spring, doing nothing at all, looking east past the harbour wall.

"Counting the days, captain?" somebody asked him, meaning it unkindly.

"Three hundred and eleven," he said.

He turned the empty glass over under his shirt. He had no ship, no bond, no witness and no evidence, and rule six is rule six: a year to the day, at the same place. He was not going to be there. He had no way to get there, and he found, sitting on the bollard in the good coat that was too good for a ship he no longer had, that he did not need to go.

She would come up. Whether or not anybody was watching.
