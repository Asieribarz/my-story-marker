They came onto the Restinga an hour after dawn on the nineteenth, and the sea changed colour under the bow the way it always does at the edge: green, then a green so pale it is almost no colour, and then the bottom, two fathoms down and lying there in the light like something in a shop window. Sand. Lanes of weed going away west. The black ribs of a ship nobody had ever named.

Ignacio had the lead going and did not need it.

Pau was at the crosstrees because he was light and because he had the best ears aboard, and at twenty minutes past the hour he stopped calling the water and went quiet, and then he came down the shrouds so fast he took the skin off both hands.

He did not go to Ignacio. He went to Brites, which told everybody something.

"It is not in my ears," he said.

"Where is it."

He put his hand flat on his own breastbone, and then, not finding that enough, he knocked on it twice with his knuckles. "Here. It is in the bone. It is in my teeth. I can hear the ship and the wind at the same time and it is *under* them." His face had gone the colour of the sand down there. "I thought it was a story. I have been telling people it was a story. I am sorry, captain."

Nobody spoke. Fausto had come up from below with his hat on and was holding the mizzen shrouds with both hands and looking at the boy the way a man looks at a bill.

And Ignacio found that the thing he felt was not triumph, and was not fear, and had no name he knew: eleven years of being the only person aboard any ship who believed it, ended, in the time it took a boy to knock on his own chest. There was nothing left to argue about. There was only the work.

"Both boats down," he said. "Hair-rope in the bow of each. Brites, the second boat is yours."

Brites went to the rail, unpicked the plait she had worn for fifteen years, cut it off at the nape with the knife she never used for anything, and handed it forward to be woven in.

"Why?" Pau said.

"Because we are short," she said, and her hands were not steady, and she did not say why they were not.
