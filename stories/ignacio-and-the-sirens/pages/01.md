The rope-walk behind the fish market sold hair by weight and asked no questions, which was the only reason Ignacio Robles had used it for eleven years.

"Nine pounds," the woman said. "There is no more in Cadiz. There is no more in Sanlucar either, before you walk there."

"Nine pounds is a hundred and twenty feet."

"Then you have a hundred and twenty feet."

He paid her and carried the coil out into the white light off the harbour wall, where Pau was sitting on the bollard with his legs swinging, and Don Fausto Delgado was standing in the shade of the bond office with his hat on, sixty years old and already looking at the water as if it had insulted him.

"Captain." Fausto had the ledger under his arm, the one with the brass lock. "Before you put that aboard."

He unfolded the bond on the top of a fish crate and held it flat with two fingers against the wind. Ignacio had read it twice already. He read the third clause again anyway, because Fausto had stopped there with his thumb.

*A living specimen, delivered whole and breathing to a place of the financier's choosing, before witnesses of the financier's choosing.*

"Living," Ignacio said.

"Living. A dead one is a fish, and I have seen four fish in twenty years and paid for two of them." Fausto folded the paper along its old creases. "You have had three commissions from three men. Each of them you came back to with a story. I am not buying a story. I have been laughed at once and it was enough."

"Understood."

"I do not think it is. So." He put the ledger back under his arm. "I am sailing with you."

Pau's legs stopped swinging.

"There is no cabin," Ignacio said.

"I shall have yours, and you shall have the deck, and neither of us will enjoy it." Fausto was already walking toward the slip, a dry man going carefully over wet stone. "Sixty-one days, captain. After that the bond is forfeit and the ship is mine, and I shall sell her to somebody who fishes."
