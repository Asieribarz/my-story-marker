At the bar Brites took her hands off the tiller and refused, and Fausto steered at the brown seam himself. Ignacio did not tear the bond: he folded it into the old man's pocket, conceded the ship in seventeen days, and said eleven years had not bought anything that turns one man's word into two men's, because rule four does not care about a hall in Seville. He put the helm down himself, southwest by west, four days back to the Restinga. They raised the shoal in a glass calm. She weighed nothing and the grey had gone all through her. Brites offered the knife; he took it, because she had cut her hair for the rope. He said something short that Pau would never repeat, parted the three turns at the second draw, and she went down without a sound over bright empty sand. La Alondra came into Cadiz seven days past her bond, with an empty hold. The town settled on eleven years and nothing to show. Fausto sold the ship; Brites went mate into a Genoa trader; Pau kept the cut rope sixty years. Ignacio no longer needed to be believed.
