Kael, sole ferryman of the Strait, owner of the Glass Ferry, has made thirty years of crossings without losing a passenger. Merchant Paine hired him: four times the fare, paid in a purse Kael accepted, to carry one passenger out into the Strait and never reach the far shore — no witnesses, the papers and the passenger both Paine's property. Kael agreed; refusal would have made Paine an enemy.

The passenger is Vex — lean, dark clothes, carrying a bundle of documents, boarded unasked. Orin, Kael's young, talkative deckhand, noticed Vex was bleeding: a bound wound Vex says will hold three hours, long enough to reach a sanctuary on the far shore whose people can close it. Vex knows Paine's men have waited on the near quay since dark and will kill Vex on the sand if the boat returns; Vex does not know Kael was paid to drown them.

Orin does not know about the purse, which is in the cabin locker. The crossing began on the night tide; Vex had been aboard over half an hour when the bleeding was found. Kael has refused to turn back and set the tiller for the deep channel, intending to arrive.
