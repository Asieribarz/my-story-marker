The water changed an hour out.

Kael felt it first through the tiller, the way a man feels a fever in another man's hand. The long easy swells that had carried them out of the harbor mouth broke apart into something short and crossed and angry, and the Glass Ferry began to shudder along her whole length, timber speaking to timber from prow to stern.

"Orin. Sheet in. Half a hand, no more."

"Half a hand." The boy was already moving, and for once he did not ask why. He hauled, snubbed the line, and looked back for the nod.

Out of the mist to starboard came a sound like cloth tearing: water breaking white over the rocks at the Strait's heart. Kael did not look at it. He had never once needed to look at it. He leaned on the tiller and put the bow a little across the run of the current, feeling for the seam of deep water that ran between the rocks and the shoal, the channel he had found by feel when he was younger than Orin and had been finding ever since.

The ferry heeled. Green water came over the rail and swept the deck ankle-deep, and Orin lost his footing on the wet oak and went down hard against the cabin.

A hand caught him by the collar before the next roll could take him further. Vex. Kneeling, braced, holding the bundle of documents clear under one arm and the boy with the other, face as still as a shut door.

"The rope," Vex said. "Behind you. Take a turn round your waist."

Orin took it. He was shaking, and he did it anyway, and then he reached back and hauled on the sheet with Vex's weight added to his own, the two of them working the line together while the deck tilted and the mist poured past.

"Hold her there," Kael said. "Both of you. Hold her there."

They held. The Glass Ferry went through the crossed water the way she had gone through it for fifty years, and came out the other side into swells long and slow and almost gentle, and the sound of the rocks fell away behind them.

Nobody spoke for a while. Orin was still holding the rope; Vex was still holding Orin. Somewhere ahead, close now, the mist carried the smell of sand.
