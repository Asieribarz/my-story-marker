# World Rules

1. The Strait is treacherous and cannot be navigated by ordinary means: its currents shift without warning, its waters run deep and cold, and the rocks at its heart are hidden by perpetual mist. Only a ferryman who has sailed it for decades can read the water by feel.

2. The Glass Ferry is old but unbreakable: it has carried passengers across the Strait for fifty years without sinking, and Kael knows every beam and plank of it as if it were part of his own body. No other boat attempts this passage.

3. Once a ferry enters the Strait, there is no stopping and no turning back until one shore is reached: the current and the rocks make retreat impossible. The crossing takes between two and four hours depending on the water's mood.

4. Contracts and loyalty are the law of this place: official authorities are distant and the Strait is beyond their reach. What two people agree to do in the mist is binding forever, and violations have consequences only money and reputation can measure.

5. The mist that rises from the Strait never fully lifts: it obscures the far shore until the moment the ferry's hull touches sand, and it conceals what happens in the passage from any witness on either bank.

6. A ferryman's reputation is his only currency: thirty years of safe passages have made Kael trusted by merchants, nobles, and fugitives alike. Once that reputation is broken, no passenger will ever board his boat again, and other ferrymen will refuse to acknowledge him.

7. The passenger cannot be told of any arrangement between the ferryman and those who contract for the crossing: deception is part of the compact, and a passenger who suspects betrayal becomes unpredictable and dangerous to the boat.
