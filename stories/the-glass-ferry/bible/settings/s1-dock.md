name: The Ferry Dock
---

**Appearance.** Small—barely more than a stone shelf worn smooth by thirty years of footsteps. A wooden structure, weathered gray, holds rope and tools. The Glass Ferry bobs at its mooring, a small vessel with a high prow and a cabin barely large enough for two people to stand inside. The dock sits at the near shore of the Strait, at the point where the land rises steeply and the mist begins to thicken. The water at the dock is calm, almost glassy, but beyond it the surface grows textured and unpredictable. The far shore is invisible from the dock; it is always concealed by the mist and the distance.

**Atmosphere.** One of threshold and waiting. Passengers arrive with tension in their shoulders, aware that they are about to entrust their lives to a stranger and a small boat. The dock is where the Strait is still negotiable, still optional, still reversible. Once the boat leaves the dock, the Strait itself takes over.

**Function.** The liminal space where passengers commit themselves to the crossing and where Kael receives his commissions. It is private and isolated, patrolled by no authority, which is why merchants and nobles pay for its discretion.
